Custom Desk & Bookcase

A custom desk and integrated bookcase designed in Onshape and subsequently manufactured from panel-based furniture components. The project included the complete CAD layout, component sizing, connection planning, and a dedicated system of 3D printed drilling jigs for accurate and repeatable positioning of dowels and fasteners.

Outcome Manufactured

Project Overview

The objective was to design a large custom workstation and bookcase specifically for the available room space. The furniture was modelled as a complete assembly in Onshape before manufacture, allowing the overall proportions, storage arrangement, clearances, and component relationships to be evaluated before material was cut.

The final furniture closely follows the original CAD design. Some details, particularly the drawer implementation, were modified during manufacture while preserving the intended overall geometry, storage layout, and function.

Design Goals

  • Maximize usable desk and storage space within the available room.
  • Integrate the desk and bookcase into one coordinated design.
  • Use practical and readily manufacturable panel construction.
  • Create repeatable connections using dowels and furniture fasteners.
  • Reduce dependence on repeated manual measurement during drilling.
  • Validate dimensions and component relationships in CAD before manufacture.

CAD Development

The complete design was developed in Onshape as an assembly of individual furniture components. Working with the full assembly made it possible to check the relationship between shelves, desk surfaces, vertical panels, storage spaces, and connection locations before committing to manufacture.

Connection Strategy

The furniture was designed around repeatable panel connections using dowels and mechanical furniture fasteners. The corresponding hole positions were defined during the CAD design so that mating components could be aligned consistently during construction.

Because the accuracy of these holes directly affected the final alignment of the furniture, dedicated drilling jigs were developed rather than relying exclusively on repeated manual measurement.

Manufacturing Challenge

A large number of connection holes had to be reproduced accurately across multiple panels. Small errors in position could accumulate during assembly and affect alignment over the full length of the desk and bookcase.

Several jig geometries were therefore developed to suit different panel locations and connection configurations while transferring the intended hole positions from the CAD design to the physical parts.

3D Printed Drill Jig System

The drilling jigs were designed in Onshape and manufactured using FDM 3D printing. Multiple variants were required because different panels and connection locations presented different geometric constraints.

The jig system provided physical references for drilling, improving repeatability while reducing the number of dimensions that had to be manually transferred to each panel.

Jig System Overview

Alignment Feature

Selected jigs include a narrow alignment slit. After the required position was measured and marked on the wooden panel, the reference mark could be visually aligned through the slit before drilling.

This provided a simple and direct way to combine careful measurement with repeatable jig positioning without obscuring the reference mark.

Corner Relief for Reliable Positioning

The relieved corner visible on selected jigs is intentional. A sharp theoretical 90° internal corner is not always reproduced perfectly by FDM printing, and the wooden panel itself may also contain a small edge radius or cutting imperfection.

Removing material from this region prevents the corner from becoming an unintended contact point. The jig can therefore locate against the intended reference surfaces more reliably while also reducing printed material.

Drilling Process

During manufacture, the jig was positioned against the relevant panel references and used to guide the drilling operation. Drill depth was controlled separately using drill stops to avoid drilling deeper than required.

Connections & Hardware

Dowels and mechanical furniture fasteners were used to locate and join the individual panels. Their positions were coordinated with the CAD design and the corresponding drilling jigs.

Technical Drawing

A representative panel drawing is included to show how the design geometry and hole locations were defined for manufacture.

From CAD to Finished Product

The completed furniture preserves the principal geometry, shelving arrangement, workstation layout, and overall proportions established in the CAD model.

The drawer construction changed during manufacture, representing a practical design iteration between the original digital model and the finished product while maintaining the intended function.
