Custom Desk & Bookcase

A complete design-to-manufacture project involving the development of a custom desk and integrated bookcase, CAD modelling, furniture connection strategy, manufacturing preparation, and a purpose-built system of 3D-printed drilling guides.

Project Overview

The objective was to design and manufacture a large custom desk and bookcase specifically for the available room while maximizing usable work surface and storage space.

The project was developed in CAD before manufacturing so that the dimensions, panel geometry, shelf positions, interfaces, and connection locations could be planned before material was cut.

Because the furniture consisted of many individually manufactured panels, repeatable hole positioning became one of the most important manufacturing challenges. To solve this, I designed and 3D printed a dedicated family of drilling jigs.

Manufacturing Challenge

A major challenge was accurately reproducing the large number of connection holes required across different panels.

Measuring and positioning every hole independently would increase manufacturing time and introduce cumulative positioning errors. Commercial drilling templates were also poorly suited to the specific geometry of the design.

I therefore developed a dedicated set of 3D-printed drilling guides matched to the furniture geometry and connection system.

3D-Printed Drill Guide System

Multiple jigs were required because different panels and connection locations used different hole patterns.

The guides locate directly against the panel geometry and use metal drill bushings to constrain the drill bit. This provides repeatable positioning while protecting the printed components from direct drill-bit wear.

Jig CAD Development

Rather than creating one universal fixture, several purpose-built guides were designed around the different drilling operations. This kept each tool simple while allowing the required hole locations to be reproduced consistently.

Multi-Hole Guide

Larger guide designed to control several related hole locations from common panel reference surfaces.

Compact Guide

Smaller guide for localized drilling operations where the larger fixtures could not be used efficiently.

Straight Guide

Dedicated linear guide for repeatable hole positioning along panel edges and reference lines.

Alignment by Reference Mark

Some drilling locations could not be referenced conveniently from an external panel edge. For these operations, the jig incorporates a narrow alignment slit.

After the hole position is carefully measured and marked on the panel, the slit allows the jig to be aligned directly with the reference mark before drilling.

Corner Relief for Reliable Location

The apparently missing corner on several jigs is intentional.

Perfectly sharp internal 90° corners are difficult to reproduce consistently with FDM printing, and the manufactured wooden panel edges may also contain small deviations.

Removing material from the internal corner prevents the jig from locating on an imperfect corner. Instead, it references the two functional panel surfaces, improving repeatability while also reducing unnecessary printed material.

Controlled Drilling Depth

Drill stops were used together with the printed guides to control drilling depth and reduce the risk of drilling through the furniture panels.

This was particularly important for blind holes used by dowels and furniture connectors.

Jig + Depth Stop

The drilling jig controls position and angle while the mechanical stop controls penetration depth.

Drilling Tools

Different drill diameters and depth settings were used for the various connection features.

Connection Hardware

The furniture uses dowels and mechanical furniture connectors. Their hole locations had to correspond accurately between mating panels, making repeatable drilling essential to successful assembly.

Engineering Outcome

This project combined mechanical design, manufacturing planning, manual fabrication, additive manufacturing, and practical problem-solving in a single design-to-production workflow.

The drilling fixtures transformed repetitive manual measurement into a controlled and repeatable manufacturing process. More importantly, the tooling was designed around the actual manufacturing constraints rather than treating 3D printing as an isolated part of the project.
